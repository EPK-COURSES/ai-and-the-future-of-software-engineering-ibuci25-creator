## **Personal Reflection — AI and the Future of Software Engineering**

**Topic 8: Real Industry Case Studies**

### **What surprised me most?**

Before I did this research, I thought the main idea was that AI was replacing developers, but what surprised me was the fact that it was absolutely the opposite, where companies like Dualingo and Microsoft are keeping their engineers and, in some cases, are hiring more while increasing outputs. One example was Duolingo, which, with the same number of employees, can now produce four to five times more content in the same period of time. Another thing is that the AI shift that is happening is a human-centered change; for example, at Shopify, engineers are evaluated not on how AI writes code but on how they are able to guide and manage AI.

\---

### **What concerns me about AI in software engineering?**

* A concern is that dependence on AI can weaken basic skills; for example, if developers generate code that they do not fully understand, they may create a dependence on AI that becomes dangerous if the tools fail or produce incorrect results. Some reports show that users develop a dependence on tools like GitHub Copilot, affecting their workflow.
* The Duolingo case shows how important communication is, because when the company published the AI-first strategy, many people were worried about their jobs, so even if the goal is productivity, unclear messages can create distrust.
* As we saw in the research from Google, AI does not fix the weak practices of engineers but amplifies them so that strong teams become stronger and the weak ones become more chaotic.

\---

### **What opportunities do I see?**

One of the best opportunities is to focus more on meaningful engineering work. If AI can do tasks like documentation, writing code, and simple tests, engineers can focus more on architecture, design, and problem-solving. Another opportunity is to treat collaboration with AI as a real skill; for example, companies like Shopify already value engineers who can plan, break down problems, and lead AI systems.

### **Which skills will remain important?**

* **Systems thinking and architecture**
* **Critical evaluation**
* **Communication**
* **Adaptability**

\---

### **Did my opinion change after research?**

Yes. I initially thought that AI would make programming faster, but after studying some companies, I now see that AI will change what engineers do rather than how quickly they do it. My opinion is that, despite the fact that job replacement is a worrying reality, in these case studies it is more mixed, with increased productivity, changes in roles, and development of companies, not just the replacement of employees.

\---

### **How will I personally adapt?**

As a start, I want to strengthen the basic skills where I need to understand how code is written, not just generate it. This means practicing problem solving without relying on AI. Secondly, I want to learn how to use AI intentionally, not as a shortcut, but as a tool that will help me improve and increase productivity. Thirdly, I want to be curious and continue learning based largely on successful companies because they experimented and shared knowledge.

\---

### **Final thought**

Software engineering is still a fundamental discipline where, even though AI can change the tools and workflow, judgment, creativity, and responsibility are essential.
